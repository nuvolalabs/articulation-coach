# Articulation Training for Corporate Leaders — Research & Product Spec

*Goal: an app whose user measurably improves articulation in **3 months of daily use**,
and can "speak like a leader" in corporate meetings and sales pitches.*

This document has two halves. **Part 1** is the research (what actually works, and the
honest evidence on timelines). **Part 2** is the product spec that turns that research
into a buildable app. Sources are numbered and listed at the end.

---

## TL;DR / verdict

- **The 3-month goal is realistic — but only if the training is a *motor-skill* program,
  not a content course.** Articulation is how precisely you shape speech sounds with your
  lips, tongue, jaw and soft palate; it is a physical skill, so it responds to short,
  frequent, feedback-driven practice, not to lectures.[1][10]
- **Expect this arc:** you *hear* a difference in your own recordings in **2–4 weeks**;
  other people notice in **4–6 weeks**; the new patterns become **largely automatic by
  month 3**.[2][3][5] That matches habit-formation research: automaticity took a median of
  **66 days** (range 18–254) in the classic Lally et al. study.[37]
- **The four levers that decide success** (all well-supported): daily *short* sessions,
  **recorded feedback** (you cannot hear your own errors in real time), **specificity**
  (drill your weak sounds, not "speak more clearly"), and **transfer** into real
  speaking.[2][5][16]
- **The market gap:** today's apps (Orai, Speeko, Yoodli, Poised) are *broad public-speaking
  coaches* — pace, fillers, confidence — and the closest articulation trainer (Articulated)
  is general-purpose.[19][21][27] **Nobody owns "the articulation gym for professionals":
  a structured, phoneme-aware, 3-month motor-skill program with before/after proof and
  corporate-meeting + sales-pitch transfer scenarios.** That is the wedge.

# PART 1 — RESEARCH

## 1. What "articulation" actually is (and why most apps miss it)

Articulation is the **precision with which you shape sounds** — lips, tongue, jaw, soft
palate — plus the pace, pauses and breath that carry them. It is distinct from *content*
(what you say) and from *accent* (which sounds your first language trained).[1][10]

Because it is a **motor skill**, adults can change it — the brain retains the capacity to
re-model motor patterns throughout life.[2] That reframes the whole product: this is
training a muscle, and it obeys motor-learning laws:

- **Power law of practice** — improvement is fast at first, then slows. The first two weeks
  produce the most visible change.[2]
- **Feedback accelerates learning** — practising with audio/visual feedback beats practice
  without it, *because speakers cannot accurately perceive their own articulation in real
  time* (the brain "fills in" expected sounds).[2] This is the single most important design
  principle for the app.
- **Specificity** — general "speak clearly" practice produces vague results; targeted work
  on specific weak sounds produces measurable change in days.[2]
- **Random/variable practice + fading feedback** produce better *retention and transfer*
  than blocked, constant-feedback drilling.[18]
- **Deliberate practice** — training focused on specific sub-tasks with immediate feedback
  and repeated performance — is the engine of expert performance.[16] A deliberate-practice
  public-speaking curriculum measurably raised residents' ratings over their training.[17]

## 2. The honest timeline (what to promise the user)

| Milestone | Typical timing | Source |
|---|---|---|
| You hear the difference in your own recordings | **2–4 weeks** | [2][3] |
| Colleagues/friends notice you sound "clearer" | **4–6 weeks** | [2][3] |
| Measurable clarity gains from 15–30 min daily sessions | **4–6 weeks** | [5] |
| New patterns feel automatic | **~Month 3** | [2] |
| Habit automaticity (median, all habit types) | **66 days** | [37] |

Progress map that recurs across sources: **Week 1** you become *aware* (recordings often
sound *worse* — you're hearing problems you used to filter out); **Week 2** targeted sounds
improve inconsistently; **Weeks 3–4** improvement holds in casual speech too; **Weeks 5–6**
it feels automatic and others comment; **Month 3+** largely automatic, with diminishing
returns that periodic review maintains.[2]

**Decay warning:** gaps longer than ~48 hours cause motor-skill decay and a longer warm-up
before fluent articulation returns — so *daily short* beats *occasional long* by a wide
margin.[5]

## 3. The evidence-based training system

### 3.1 The core loop — record, review, repeat

Every credible source converges on one loop: **speak out loud → analyse the attempt →
choose ONE change → try again.**[2][8][28] The app must make this loop frictionless and
must make the "one change" concrete ("your final *t* is dropping — re-read and hit every
ending"). A generic score is useless; a specific, actionable target is the product.

### 3.2 The daily 10-minute session (structure that recurs everywhere)

A widely-used pattern is a 10-minute routine: **~1 min breath → ~3 min mouth warm-ups →
~3 min precision reading drills → ~3 min real speaking + record.**[4] A 12-week scaffold
in the same literature: weeks 1–2 one target sentence daily; weeks 3–6 rotate three
sentences, 5 sessions/week; weeks 7–10 add variable speed and short paragraphs; weeks
11–12 two recorded "public tests" and lock the habit.[6]

### 3.3 The exercise library (by layer)

**Breath (fuel):** box breathing (in-4/hold-4/out-4/hold-4) and hissing exhale ("sss") to
train a steady air stream that keeps sentence endings strong.[4] Diaphragmatic
(belly) breathing is the foundation of a stronger, more resonant, more *authoritative*
voice — breathe in through the nose, speak as the belly comes back in.[41][42][44]

**Warm-ups (articulators):** jaw release (hang open, side to side), lip trills, "ee–oo"
stretches, tongue circles, and the classic **"pa-ta-ka" / "ba-da-ga"** consonant drills
(lips → tongue tip → back of tongue), slow then fast.[4]

**Precision drills (the meat):**
- **Tongue twisters** — *not* for speed. Make every consonant pop while each syllable stays
  distinct; speed up only when every sound stays crisp.[3] An Edinburgh study found 5 min
  daily tongue-twister practice over 3 weeks produced a **42% improvement in /θ/ and /ð/
  accuracy** vs. a flashcard control.[7] Use phoneme-targeted twisters (e.g. "red lorry,
  yellow lorry" for /r/ vs /l/).[7]
- **The Last-Sound Technique** — make the final sound of every word audible ("We need**d**
  to submi**t** the repor**t** by Friday"). Most mumbling is dropped endings.[2]
- **Pencil/pen resistance** — bite gently on a pen and read aloud ~2 min; on removal
  articulation is measurably sharper.[3][4]
- **Over-articulated reading** — exaggerate every mouth movement as if someone across the
  room is lip-reading.[4]
- **Ending-sound reading** — read again focusing only on the last sound of each word.[4]
- **Exaggerated reading / "my mouth makes many mobile movements"** — open wider than feels
  natural, exaggerating consonant contact.[11]
- **Shadowing** — play a short native monologue and repeat *simultaneously* without pausing,
  copying rhythm, stress and intonation.[12][13] A systematic review supports it for
  intelligibility and connected speech.[14][15]

### 3.4 Measure the right things

Concrete, trackable metrics that recur in the literature: **minutes practised, repetitions
completed, and error count per set** (e.g. 10 min/day ≈ 24 reps, target a 30% error-count
drop in 2 weeks).[6] Delivery metrics used by the apps: **words per minute / pace**
(~145 WPM reads as easy to follow), **filler-word count**, **pauses**, **intonation/pitch
variety**, and a **clarity/articulation score**.[22][28] Phoneme-level accuracy is the
SLP-grade metric the consumer apps mostly *don't* surface.[1][2]

### 3.5 Progression, spacing and transfer

Rotate targets every few days to avoid "fossilising" an error; randomise drill order and
fade feedback over time to build retention.[7][18] Crucially, **transfer to real speech
must be designed in** — practise in the setting where you actually speak (at a desk,
standing, with room noise), use cold prompts, timed answers, mid-thought restarts ("Let me
say that more clearly"), and high-stakes rehearsal.[9][18]

### 3.6 The corporate / leader layer (the "speak like a leader" part)

Articulation alone isn't enough; leaders also need **presence, structure and persuasion**.
Evidence-backed pieces:

- **Voice/presence (Roger Love method):** breathe from the diaphragm; avoid breathy "air"
  in business settings (it reads as weak); stop using descending pitch at sentence ends —
  go *up* on the last word before a comma/period to sound confident and authoritative.[41][42][44]
  Love's method — diaphragmatic breathing, daily vocal exercises, and bridging head and
  chest voice via the "middle voice" — has been used to coach executives and public
  figures.[43]
- **Structure:** Toastmasters' **PREP (Point → Reason → Example → Point)** and "be
  succinct, make every word count"; "speak to groups as individuals"; rehearse aloud and
  time it.[48][49]
- **Concision & messaging:** *Smart Brevity* (say more with less; ~26-second attention
  window) and *Made to Stick* (make ideas memorable: simple, unexpected, concrete,
  credible, emotional, story).[33]
- **Persuasion/pitch structure:** the **Challenger** arc (Warmer → Reframe → Rational
  Drowning → Emotional Impact → A New Way → Your Solution) built on *Teach, Tailor, Take
  control, Constructive tension*[45][47]; **SPIN** (Situation → Problem → Implication →
  Need-payoff) and **SNAP** (Simple, iNvaluable, Align, Prioritise) as alternative
  discovery-led structures.[46]

## 6. Habit design (so people actually finish 3 months)

The research on streaks is blunt and directly relevant to a 90-day program:

- **Most streaks die early** — median daily streak across a 1.5M-user dataset is just
  **4 days**; the make-or-break window is days 3–7. Design for *early* survival.[36]
- **Loss aversion is the engine** — Kahneman/Tversky prospect theory: losses hurt ~2× more
  than equal gains; a streak turns an abstract habit into a concrete thing you can lose.[37]
- **The 66-day rule** — automaticity median 66 days, so make 66 (not 30) the "habit formed"
  milestone.[37]
- **Minimum viable commitment** — define an absurdly low "counts as done" bar (one drill,
  one rep) so a bad day never breaks the chain; lower minimums → higher long-term
  engagement.[37]
- **Forgiving streaks** — grace days (~1/week) lifted 30-day retention ~6–9% and rebound
  rate 15–25% in one case study; don't punish one miss.[38]
- **Compliance rate > streak count** — track "24 of 30 days" rather than a fragile
  consecutive-day counter, which turns one miss into a "quit moment".[39][40]
- **Three-phase design** — Bootstrap (days 1–14: frequent rewards, visible progress) →
  Consolidation (15–30: safety mechanisms) → Graduation (30+: shift to permanent
  achievements, reduce daily pressure).[39]
- **Proxy-metric trap** — don't let the streak measure *app opens* instead of *real
  practice*; log quality (error count, clarity), not just attendance.[36]

# PART 2 — PRODUCT SPEC

## 7. Positioning

> **"The 10-minute-a-day articulation gym for professionals. Speak clearly, sound
> authoritative, and own the room in meetings and pitches — with proof you're improving."**

- **Primary user:** the working professional who is smart but mumbles, drops word endings,
  rushes, or lacks vocal presence — especially in **corporate meetings** and **sales
  pitches** (Arun's stated use case).
- **Promise (evidence-backed):** hear a difference in **2 weeks**, others notice in
  **6 weeks**, largely automatic by **month 3**.[2][5]
- **Wedge vs. competitors:** phoneme-aware articulation drills + a structured 3-month
  motor-skill progression + corporate/sales transfer + before/after proof. Competitors do
  broad delivery metrics; ELSA does pronunciation-for-English; Articulated does general
  drills.[19][21][27]

## 8. The 3-month program (the core IP)

**Month 1 — Foundation: awareness + articulation mechanics**
- Baseline: record a 60-second "current me" clip (kept for the month-3 comparison).
- Daily: breath → warm-ups → **Last-Sound / pa-ta-ka / tongue twisters / pencil drill** →
  record + one change.[2][3][4]
- Diagnose the user's **2–3 weak sound patterns** from recordings and target them.[2]
- Metric focus: **error count per set** (target −30% by week 2).[6]

**Month 2 — Delivery: pace, pause, intonation, concision**
- Add **shadowing** (curated audio), **over-articulation**, box breathing under load.[4][12]
- Delivery metrics: **WPM/pace** (target ~140–150 for easy follow), **filler rate**,
  **pause placement**, **intonation variety** (fix descending sentence-ends).[22][42]
- Structure drills: **PREP**, lead-with-the-point (Smart Brevity), 60-second
  distillation.[11][33][48]
- Metric focus: clarity score + filler count + pace.

**Month 3 — Transfer & pressure: meetings + sales pitches**
- **Scenario roleplay** with AI personas: stand-up update, disagreeing with a manager,
  stakeholder Q&A, discovery call, objection handling, closing a pitch.[21][24]
- **Sales module:** rehearse the **Challenger** arc and **SPIN/SNAP** questioning; deliver
  and get feedback on structure *and* delivery.[45][46][47]
- **Pressure drills:** cold prompts, 90s→45s timed answers, mid-thought restarts, "record a
  real meeting recap" weekly.[9]
- **Weekly recorded "public test"** (2 in the final weeks) + a final month-3 "after" clip
  compared to the baseline.[6]

## 9. Daily session design (10 minutes, the retention unit)

| Minute | Block | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0–1 | Breath | Box breathing or hissing exhale[4] |
| 1–4 | Warm-up | Jaw/lips/tongue + pa-ta-ka + one phoneme-targeted twister[4][7] |
| 4–7 | Precision drill | Rotating: pencil, over-articulation, Last-Sound, shadowing[3][4][12] |
| 7–10 | Real speaking | Speak to a prompt → **record** → see ONE specific change → re-attempt[2][8] |

Minimum-viable day (bad days): the 0–1 breath block + one drill = streak preserved.[37]

## 10. Feature set

**MVP (ship first):**
1. **Guided daily session** with the timed 10-min structure above.
2. **Record → playback → compare** (before/after in one view) — the feedback core.[2]
3. **Speech analysis:** pace/WPM, filler words, pause count, intonation variety, and a
   **clarity/articulation score**; optional **phoneme-level accuracy** (the differentiator).
4. **Weak-sound targeting** — auto-identify 2–3 problem sounds and route drills to them.[2]
5. **Drill library** (the exercises in §3.3) with phoneme filters.
6. **3-month plan** with milestones, and a **weekly 1-minute test** + progress trend.
7. **Forgiving habit system:** compliance-rate view (not a brittle counter), 1 grace
   day/week, 66-day "habit formed" milestone.[37][38][40]
8. **Corporate/sales prompt packs** (meeting updates, pitch openings).

**Phase 2:** AI **roleplay personas** (meeting/sales) with scored feedback[21][24];
**shadowing audio library**; **sales-pitch coach** (Challenger/SPIN structure); **structure
coaching** (PREP / pyramid / Smart Brevity); vocabulary/word-choice.

**Phase 3:** real-meeting analysis (desktop/live)[25][26]; human-coach marketplace[21];
team/enterprise dashboards; localisation.

## 11. Differentiation (one line each)

- **Phoneme-aware articulation** — SLP-grade targeting nobody offers to native-speaking
  professionals.[1][2]
- **A real curriculum** — a 3-month motor-skill progression, not an open-ended library.[6]
- **Proof** — before/after audio and a clarity trend, so improvement is *visible*.[2]
- **Transfer built in** — corporate-meeting and sales-pitch scenarios, not just monologues.[9]
- **Honest, science-based messaging** — we quote the real timeline (2 wks / 6 wks / 3 mo),
  which builds trust competitors' hype erodes.[2][5]

## 12. Lean technical approach

Fits the standard lean stack (PWA + Supabase + Vercel) and defers heavy infra:

- **Client:** mobile-first **PWA** (Vite + React) so we ship to iOS/Android/web with one
  codebase and no app-store wait for the MVP; native wrapper later.
- **Audio capture:** browser `MediaRecorder`; playback + before/after compare.
- **Analysis (MVP):** transcript + timings from an ASR service (Whisper or the browser's
  Web Speech API) → compute pace, filler rate, pauses; pitch/intonation from Web Audio
  (autocorrelation) on-device.
- **Phoneme scoring (the differentiator):** evaluate a **pronunciation-assessment API**
  (e.g. Azure Speech pronunciation assessment returns accuracy/fluency/completeness +
  per-phoneme scores) — run it **server-side via a Vercel function** so the key never
  ships to the client. Flag cost early and gate it behind Pro.
- **Backend:** Supabase (auth + Postgres for sessions, scores, streaks; storage for audio);
  Vercel serverless for scoring endpoints. Anon reads + service-key writes, as per our
  usual pattern.
- **Privacy:** audio is sensitive — process/retain locally where possible, state clearly
  what leaves the device, let users delete recordings. This is a trust feature, not a
  checkbox (competitors lean on it: Speeko/Orai both promise private audio).[21][20]

## 13. Business model & metrics

- **Freemium:** the daily loop + basic metrics free (habit first); **Pro** for phoneme
  scoring, full drill library, AI roleplay, sales module, deep analytics. Competitor
  benchmarks: ~$6–30/mo, ~$50–150/yr.[19][20][22]
- **Teams/enterprise:** seat licensing for L&D/sales orgs (Yoodli/Speeko lead here).[21][24]
- **North-star metric:** % of users whose **clarity score improves ≥X% over 30 days** — the
  product's actual promise — not DAU. Pair it with **day-7 / day-30 retention** and
  **compliance rate**.[40]

## 14. Risks / open questions

- **Phoneme-scoring cost & accuracy** for native speakers (APIs are tuned for L2 English) —
  validate before making it the headline feature.
- **"Speak like a leader" is broader than articulation** — scope the MVP to articulation +
  delivery and add persuasion/structure in Phase 2 so we don't dilute the wedge.
- **ASR misreads** (names, accents) — always show the transcript and let the user correct it
  (Articulated makes the same honest caveat).[28]
- **Not a medical tool** — must state it doesn't diagnose/treat speech disorders and point
  to an SLP/clinician for clinical concerns.[1][28]
